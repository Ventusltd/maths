# Squaring

## The problem

Sometimes a measurement grows in **two directions at once**.

Imagine a square floor that is 3 metres long and 3 metres wide.

To find how much floor there is:

```
3 × 3 = 9
```

The floor has an area of **9 square metres**.

## What "squared" means

To square a number means:

> multiply the number by itself.

Examples:

```
2 squared = 2 × 2 = 4
3 squared = 3 × 3 = 9
4 squared = 4 × 4 = 16
10 squared = 10 × 10 = 100
```

The small raised 2 is just shorthand:

```
3² = 3 × 3 = 9
```

## Why care?

Squaring appears whenever two dimensions multiply together.

For a square:

```
side × side = area
```

So if the side doubles, the area does **not** merely double.

A 2 m × 2 m square has area 4 m².

A 4 m × 4 m square has area 16 m².

The side doubled, but the area became four times larger.

## Technical maths

For any number $a$:

$$
a^2 = a \times a
$$

For a square with side length $s$:

$$
A=s^2
$$

The unit is also squared. If $s$ is measured in metres, area is measured in square metres, $m^2$.

## Try it in Excel

| cell | type | expect |
|---|---|---|
| A2 | `3` | 3 |
| B2 | `=A2*A2` | 9 |
| C2 | `=A2^2` | 9 |

Put 4 in A2: B2 shows 16. Put 10: 100.

## Check it yourself

1. What is 7²? (49)
2. A 2 m square and a 6 m square. How many times bigger is the area? (36 ÷ 4 = 9 times)
3. Is 3² the same as 3 × 2? (No: 9, not 6)
