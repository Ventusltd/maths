# Area and square units

## The problem

Length tells us how far something goes in one direction.

Area tells us how much flat space it covers.

A rectangle 5 metres long and 2 metres wide covers:

```
5 × 2 = 10
```

So its area is:

```
10 square metres
```

written:

```
10 m²
```

## Why "square metres"?

Imagine covering the floor with little tiles, each exactly:

```
1 metre × 1 metre
```

Each tile is one square metre.

A 5 m × 2 m rectangle fits 10 of those tiles.

That is what 10 m² means.

## Length is not area

A length of 10 m and an area of 10 m² are different kinds of measurement.

10 m tells us a distance.

10 m² tells us an amount of flat space.

## Why care?

This distinction is essential for understanding why a circle's radius and its area do not grow at the same rate.

It is also why square roots become useful: they can reverse a squared relationship.

## Technical maths

For a rectangle:

$$
A=L\times W
$$

For a square:

$$
A=s^2
$$

Dimensional units multiply too:

$$
m\times m=m^2
$$

| B2 | `2` (width, m) | 2 |
| C2 | `=A2*B2` (area, m²) | 10 |

## Check it yourself

1. A 4 m by 3 m room: how many 1 m tiles? (12)
2. Which is bigger: 10 m or 10 m²? (Trick question: they measure different things)
3. A square of area 36 m²: how long is a side? (6 m, using a square root)
