## Let the drawing answer

Learn the Kuiper connects the notes here to an interactive drawing: square roots set distance, modulo wraps a turn, and sine and cosine connect angle and distance to X and Y. The purpose is to experiment with a small example, see what changes, then reproduce the relationship in a spreadsheet.

The existing learning path remains problem first, intuition second, formula last. Start with [square roots](../02-square-roots.md), [angles and turns](../06-angles-and-turns.md), [modulo](../07-modulo-wrap-around.md), and [coordinates](../09-coordinates-sine-cosine.md). The [placement-law note](../10-kuiper-placement-law.md) distinguishes the spreadsheet exercise from the live wafer.

That distinction matters: the exercise uses `sqrt(k)` and the live wafer uses `sqrt(k + 0.5)`. A freely chosen point can be described by radius and angle or X and Y, while a real Kuiper key determines both coordinates through its rule.

Sphere and cube experiments offer a further direction: extending the relationship between square root and area to cube root and volume, with each geometry's angular or directional rule stated separately.
